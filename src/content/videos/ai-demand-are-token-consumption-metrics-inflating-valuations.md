AI demand forecasting involves predicting the future need for artificial intelligence resources, such as processing power and data interaction units, to guide strategic investments and operational planning within the technology sector. A primary metric for this demand is "token consumption," which represents the basic unit of AI interaction. Every query posed to an AI, every response it generates, and every line of code it writes consumes a certain number of tokens. While this metric appears straightforward, a closer look reveals that current measurements of AI demand, heavily reliant on token usage, may be significantly inflated, posing risks to the sector's financial stability.

## Understanding AI Demand and Token Consumption

Tokens are the fundamental currency of interaction with large language models and other AI systems. When a user inputs a prompt or an AI generates an output, these actions are broken down into tokens, which are then processed by the underlying AI infrastructure. A simple conversational exchange might consume a few hundred tokens. However, the emergence of advanced AI agents has dramatically altered consumption patterns. These agents are designed to operate autonomously, browsing the web, writing code, and completing complex tasks in the background, often for extended periods. Such operations can burn through millions of tokens without direct, continuous user oversight. This shift from discrete, user-initiated interactions to continuous, agent-driven processes makes accurate demand forecasting challenging, akin to the difference between ordering a single car ride and having a car run errands all day on an open credit card.

## The Pitfalls of Current Demand Measurement

The current methods for tracking AI usage often fail to distinguish between genuine productivity and mere activity, leading to distorted demand signals. One significant issue is "tokenmaxxing," where companies inadvertently incentivize high token consumption rather than tangible output. Some organizations, for instance, track employee AI usage on leaderboards as a sign of adoption, rather than measuring the actual productivity gains or return on investment. This can encourage employees to use AI tools excessively, even for simple tasks that might not require the most advanced models, simply to meet usage targets. For example, an engineer might prompt an AI to generate overly verbose responses or engage in iterative, low-value tasks to inflate their token count. As one CEO noted, if a $500,000 engineer did not consume at least $250,000 worth of tokens, it would be alarming, highlighting the pressure to demonstrate high usage. This approach can lead to a situation where the goal becomes burning money, rather than achieving efficiency, as observed by companies processing AI workloads for thousands of businesses.

Another contributing factor to inflated demand is the prevalence of unlimited usage plans, particularly for consumers and, until recently, some enterprise customers. Services offering unlimited messaging for a fixed monthly fee, while appealing to users, become unsustainable as AI agents consume tokens at a much faster rate than traditional chatbots. One major AI lab, for example, started to recognize the unsustainability of this model, especially as the industry moved from simple chatbots to more complex agents. This led them to cut off popular third-party tools from using unlimited subscriptions, with one estimate suggesting a single $200 Max plan could be burning $2000-5000 in compute costs. This disconnect between a flat fee and actual resource consumption means that the perceived demand is not accurately reflected in revenue, creating a false sense of usage growth and masking the true cost of operations.

## The Costly Illusion: Overinvestment and Budget Overruns

The mismeasurement of AI demand has direct and severe financial consequences, leading to significant budget overruns and potentially unsustainable investment cycles. Companies are finding their initial AI inference budgets quickly exhausted. For example, one major technology company's CTO reported that AI coding tools had already maxed out their full-year AI budget by April. Research indicates that companies are overrunning their initial inference budgets by orders of magnitude, with AI costs on track to rival engineering headcount this year.

This perceived insatiable demand for AI resources forms the basis for massive investments across the tech ecosystem. It justifies the sale of hundreds of billions of dollars in chips by hardware manufacturers and underpins plans for data center companies to build 30 gigawatts of capacity. The biggest names in tech are pouring billions of dollars into infrastructure, all predicated on the assumption that AI usage will continue to grow exponentially. However, if a meaningful portion of this usage stems from employees gaming leaderboards, agents running inefficiently in loops, or companies simply blowing through budgets they cannot sustain, then the vast infrastructure being built to serve this demand may be sized for a number that is not real. There is zero doubt that overinvestment is occurring in the sector.

## Shifting Towards Sustainable AI Economics

Recognizing the unsustainability of current models, some key players in the AI industry are beginning to pivot towards more realistic and verifiable demand-based pricing. One major AI lab, for instance, has taken steps to move away from flat-rate plans, opting instead for per-token billing for its enterprise customers and cutting off popular third-party tools from using unlimited subscriptions. This shift attaches a new price tag to every token, forcing consumers and companies to budget more carefully and potentially leading to a pullback in unverified usage. This also encourages a culture of efficiency, where developers and users are incentivized to optimize their prompts and workflows to achieve desired outcomes with fewer tokens.

The emphasis is moving towards efficiency and genuine return on investment. Instead of merely tracking how much AI is used, companies are starting to focus on what output is generated and whether the chosen AI model is appropriate for the task. Using the most advanced model on the planet to edit a simple email, for example, is often an inefficient use of resources. By aligning costs directly with consumption, businesses are encouraged to optimize their AI workflows and select models that offer the best balance of capability and cost-effectiveness. This approach not only helps control costs but also fosters innovation in more efficient AI utilization, ensuring that AI investments translate into tangible productivity gains rather than just increased spending.

## Navigating the "Cone of Uncertainty"

The challenge of accurately forecasting AI demand is compounded by the long lead times required for infrastructure development. Building new data centers, for example, can take one to two years. This creates what some industry leaders refer to as a "cone of uncertainty," where AI companies are making billion-dollar bets on demand that has not yet fully materialized or been accurately verified. Investing too little risks losing customers to competitors, while investing too much could result in underutilized capacity and revenue shortfalls, making the underlying business math unsustainable.

Companies that prioritize verifiable demand and implement transparent, per-token billing models are better positioned to navigate this uncertainty. By understanding precisely what customers are paying for and why, they can make more informed investment decisions and build infrastructure aligned with actual, sustainable usage. This prudent approach, focusing on underpromising and overdelivering, is seen as a more viable long-term strategy in an environment where the true scale of AI demand is still being determined. The market is already beginning to sort this out, favoring companies with clean, verifiable data on customer consumption.
