Craft enters a crowded market dominated by established players such as Notion, Evernote, and Apple Notes, each with its own philosophy. Apple Notes provides native integration and simplicity, suitable for quick captures but lacking advanced organization. Evernote, while powerful for collection, has faced criticism for its interface and performance over time. Notion gained immense popularity by offering a highly customizable, database-driven workspace that serves as a versatile platform for everything from project management to personal wikis. Its strength lies in its modularity, allowing users to build almost any system imaginable.  Craft differentiates itself by aiming for a middle ground: offering more power and flexibility than simple note apps but with a focus on fluid writing, beautiful design, and a less intimidating structure than Notion. Instead of Notion's block-based, database-centric approach, Craft emphasizes document-centric organization with robust linking capabilities, 'Whiteboards,' and 'Collections' for visual grouping of content. It provides a clean interface that prioritizes readability and ease of creation, which can be appealing to users who found Notion's database management too steep a learning curve or too much friction for daily journaling and brainstorming. Its emphasis on seamless offline access and strong native app performance also sets it apart, addressing common pain points with web-first applications.
