A humanoid robot is not artificial intelligence by default. Instead, it is a physical machine built to match the human body.

Artificial intelligence serves as the digital software brain that processes information. The humanoid robot provides the motors, metal frame, and sensors.

Engineers can deploy modern AI models inside that physical frame. Then, the combined machine operates as an embodied AI system (artificial intelligence placed inside a physical body).

## What is the difference between robotics hardware and AI software?

Robotics hardware includes the physical frame, motors, and sensors of a machine. In contrast, artificial intelligence is the software code that handles reasoning, learning, and decision-making.

Physical robotics focuses on mechanical engineering and electrical design. Engineers build joints, wire actuators (motors and mechanical devices that produce movement), and balance battery packs. This work creates reliable [humanoid hardware components](/video/humanoid-hardware-what-humanoid-robots-are-made-of) that survive repeated physical strain.

According to IBM, robotics focuses on physical mechanics, sensors, and actuators. Meanwhile, IBM notes that artificial intelligence simulates human intelligence and decision-making through algorithms (step-by-step mathematical rules) and data analysis.

Artificial intelligence operates purely in the digital domain until engineers connect it to motors. Software engineers write machine learning code. They train neural networks (computer systems modeled on the human brain) on visual datasets.

They also build models that recognize objects in video streams. According to South Dakota Mines, artificial intelligence decides what actions to take by processing sensor data. South Dakota Mines adds that robotics builds the physical mechanisms that execute those decisions.

| System Attribute | Robotics Hardware | Artificial Intelligence Software |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Primary Focus | Mechanics, structural joints, and electric actuators | Mathematical weights, algorithms, and neural networks |
| Operational Duty | Exerting mechanical force and moving through space | Processing raw sensor streams and selecting choices |
| Physical Presence | Tangible metal, wiring, silicon, and lithium batteries | Digital machine code executed on computer microchips |
| Primary Output | Physical actuation, limb rotation, and spatial travel | Predictions, classifications, and motor commands |

Separating the physical chassis from the code makes it much easier to understand what these machines can actually accomplish. When a frame walks smoothly, people often assume it possesses deep intelligence, yet mechanical balance is only half the puzzle. Meaningful innovation happens when durable mechanics finally meet capable software, which is why this space is so exciting to track.

## Humanoid machines can execute tasks without artificial intelligence

Humanoid robots can operate entirely on static, prewritten code and mechanical linkages. They do this without using any artificial intelligence software.

For centuries, inventors designed elaborate clockwork machines. These machines imitated human motion through gears, pulleys, and springs. According to TechTarget, mechanical automatons and early humanoid designs date back centuries to Leonardo da Vinci.

TechTarget also traces them to George Devol's 1954 Unimate industrial arm without artificial intelligence. These classic machines moved through set physical paths. They could not sense their environment or make autonomous (independent) choices.

Modern manufacturing still relies heavily on this deterministic (fixed and predictable) approach. According to the International Organization for Standardization standard ISO 8373:2021, an industrial robot is an automatically controlled, multipurpose manipulator programmable in three or more axes. This global standard defines the machine strictly by physical movement rather than by machine intelligence.

According to Deloitte, traditional robots follow deterministic instructions without machine intelligence. This means they repeat fixed motor paths and fail if an obstacle appears. If a part sits away from its expected tray, a blind script attempts to grasp empty air.

According to arXiv research published in August 2025 by Longbing Cao, humanoid robots originated six decades ago. Longbing Cao notes they began primarily as mechanical replicas of the human form rather than intelligent computational agents.

Traditional non-AI automation relies on specific structural characteristics:

- Fixed motor trajectories (preset movement paths) that blindly repeat exact joint coordinates
- Reliance on physical jigs (alignment guides), mechanical stops, and conveyor alignments
- Complete operational failure when an unexpected physical obstacle blocks the path

## How does embodied AI connect physical bodies to software?

Embodied AI connects physical robot bodies to intelligent software. It links real-time sensor streams directly to machine learning models that generate motor actions.

A computer vision model running on a desktop computer can identify a coffee mug in a photograph. However, it cannot reach out and grab it.

Embodied AI places that model inside a physical chassis (body frame) equipped with depth cameras, touch sensors, and motorized fingers. According to TechTarget, modern embodied AI represents the convergence where digital intelligence is deployed directly inside physical bodies to interact with the real world.

According to Barclays Investment Bank, the humanoid form factor provides physical utility. According to Barclays Investment Bank, 33% of global jobs require lifting and 53% require fine manipulation. Barclays Investment Bank reports that large multimodal AI models supply cognitive reasoning.

These models are software systems that can process multiple types of information, such as text, video, and sensor data. Barclays Investment Bank notes this reasoning lets robots switch between varied manual tasks without redesigning the physical factory floor.

Safety remains the central hurdle when deploying high-power machines alongside human workers. According to the International Federation of Robotics, artificial intelligence adoption in physical robotics proceeds at a narrower pace than commonly assumed. The International Federation of Robotics states that operational viability remains constrained by physical engineering factors like battery endurance, safety standards, and bipedal balance.

Industrial robot arms operate behind locked safety cages. However, mobile bipedal (two-legged) machines must share aisles with people. Pressure-sensitive grippers and software velocity limiters help reduce [humanoid robot safety risks](/video/can-humanoid-robots-be-dangerous-real-safety-risks) during close collaborative work.

## Generative foundation models provide real-time perception and task reasoning

Generative foundation models allow humanoid robots to understand spoken instructions. They also analyze visual camera inputs and dynamically plan complex physical actions.

Early robots required developers to program every single movement axis by hand. In contrast, modern foundation models process text, video, and force-sensor readings together inside unified neural networks.

According to arXiv research published in August 2025 by Longbing Cao, generative AI and multimodal large language models are integrating. Longbing Cao notes this integration is transitioning robots from mechanical replicas into interactive AI humanoids.

Processing these massive models requires specialized hardware both in the data center and inside the robot chassis. According to [NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/use-cases/humanoid-robots/), humanoid robots require a three-computer AI architecture to achieve full physical autonomy. NVIDIA defines these three specific computing layers as:

1. High-throughput AI supercomputers to train multimodal foundation models on petabytes of human video
2. Deterministic physics simulators to validate balance, walking gaits, and safety policies across virtual spaces
3. Onboard system-on-chip processors (microchips combining multiple computing components) like Jetson Thor. These processors execute real-time physical AI inference (when a trained AI model processes live data to make decisions) directly on the robot

For warehouse workers and assembly technicians, foundation models mean robots will no longer remain trapped behind warning fences. Instead, human staff will soon give plain voice commands to mobile machines. These machines will perceive loose tools, move around dropped pallets, and safely hand over heavy components.

## NextGen Humanoids reveals Figure's secret Blackbox lab and AI testing

NextGen Humanoids reviews how robotics startup Figure tests foundation models inside its secret Blackbox facility. This testing evaluates autonomous humanoid capabilities in simulated home settings.

NextGen Humanoids reports that Figure built a dedicated testing structure painted completely black inside its San Jose headquarters, named the Blackbox lab. In that facility, CEO Brett Adcock tested the startup's newest machine learning model on household chores.

NextGen Humanoids states that Brett Adcock shared these claims during an all-hands meeting released on October 22nd. He claimed the breakthrough felt like Baby AGI (an early form of artificial general intelligence capable of human-level reasoning).

The physical machinery showed measurable operational endurance during public trials. NextGen Humanoids reports that Figure 03 robots sorted packages for 200 hours without a failure. This was shown on a live stream watched by 9.3 million viewers.

NextGen Humanoids notes that one unit kept working through heat reaching 140°F inside an uncooled Los Angeles facility. NextGen Humanoids reports that Figure 03's production expenses decreased by more than 90% compared to the earlier generation.

Data collection drives the machine learning performance behind these trials. NextGen Humanoids reports that Figure spent $8.1 million in a single week on its Index program.

This program collected 81 minutes of video every second from 210,000 contributors wearing cameras. NextGen Humanoids states that Figure tested robots in 30 unfamiliar homes. They reached a 56% completion rate with video pre-training versus 9% without it.

NextGen Humanoids reports that Figure entered into a $3.5 billion agreement with cloud computing provider Nscale. The deal covers a maximum of 100,000 Nvidia processors to expand Figure's computing infrastructure and train its AI models.

NextGen Humanoids notes that Elon Musk expects Tesla customer deliveries around the end of 2027. NextGen Humanoids notes Elon Musk also expects the AI5 chip to reach Optimus around April 2027. Figure's reliance on human video contrasts with Tesla's strategy.

Tesla collects data through fleet scale across its automotive factories. Hardware iteration (rapid design updates) moves quickly across both companies. However, real-world utility still depends entirely on whether software models can reliably handle unscripted physical work.

Controlled indoor trials are a helpful starting point, but I once drove past a robot being tested right out on an open street. Seeing how the machine handled unpredictable outdoor pavement was incredible to witness firsthand. Synthetic rooms help tune baseline code, yet unpredictable everyday surroundings will ultimately prove whether these models can stand on their own.
