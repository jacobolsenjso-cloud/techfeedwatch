Saudi Arabia is actively cultivating a strong financial technology sector, directly supporting its Vision 2030 goals for economic diversification. This strategic push involves government-backed efforts and private sector partnerships to foster innovation across various financial technology domains. The Kingdom aims to establish itself as a leading hub for financial technology in the Middle East and North Africa.

## Saudi Arabia's Vision for Fintech Innovation

The Kingdom's drive into fintech is a core part of its Vision 2030, a plan to reduce reliance on oil and build a diversified, knowledge-based economy. Efforts like Fintech Saudi, led by the Saudi Central Bank and the Capital Markets Authority, are central to this transformation. Their primary goal is to support the responsible growth of fintech companies within the Saudi market. This concerted effort has positioned Saudi Arabia as the world's fastest-growing fintech market. Over the last two years, the value of fintech transactions has increased by about 20% annually. This transaction value is expected to grow further, underscoring the rapid expansion and potential of the sector.

## Accelerator Programs Fueling Startup Ecosystem

A key mechanism for fostering this growth is the Fintech Accelerator program, an initiative by Fintech Saudi powered by Flat6Labs. Flat6Labs is a major regional seed investor and startup accelerator, having invested in over 300 startups with considerable funding. It has also secured additional funding, boasts a network of over 1000 mentors and coaches, and has contributed to the creation of over 8000 jobs.

The accelerator program attracts large interest, receiving over 400 applications for a single cycle. These programs provide intensive support to selected startups. This includes over 50 sessions, totaling more than 150 hours, delivered by over 50 mentors and coaches. Engagement from over 30 partners also supports the participating companies. A typical cohort consists of about 10 innovative startups, covering a wide array of fintech areas. These include personal finance management, lending, open banking, and securities offerings.

## Innovating Digital Payments and Personal Finance

Several startups emerging from these programs are directly addressing consumer needs in Saudi Arabia. Falus, for instance, focuses on direct peer-to-peer (P2P) consumer payments. The Saudi market has a strong reliance on cross-border payments, but domestic P2P solutions have often been less convenient or user-friendly. Falus aims to simplify this by offering a mobile application that allows users to send and receive money without needing to top up funds or know the recipient's bank account details. It uses a social feed approach, and a flat 2% fee is charged to the payer for each transaction. The digital payments industry in Saudi Arabia is a large market, with the fintech segment growing by 18% year-on-year. A major portion of the Saudi population, 70% of whom are under 35, represents a key demographic for such mobile-first solutions.

Another innovator, Mela, addresses personal finance management. Saudi nationals currently have a savings ratio of 2.4%, which is considerably lower than the 10% global average. Mela's app helps users track expenses automatically, categorize transactions, and provides analytics. While core features are free, advanced analytics are available through premium subscriptions. With 72% of the Saudi population holding a bank account, there is a broad potential user base. Mela plans to expand its services to include a behavioral risk profiling system for lending by the second quarter of 2021 and an investment platform by 2022.

## Transforming Capital Markets and Investment Access

The accelerator programs also support startups modernizing capital markets and alternative funding. Withhack Capital Markets, for example, focuses on improving the efficiency of the Sukuk market. Sukuk are asset-backed or asset-based fixed-income securities that generate actual returns, differing from traditional interest-bearing bonds. The global Sukuk market has about $0.5 trillion outstanding and sees many new issuances annually. This market is expected to grow three to four times in the next five years. Withhack offers a unified platform for the entire Sukuk value chain, from issuance and distribution to servicing and administration, automating complex processes. It connects investors directly to issuances and provides custody and secondary market services. The company is regulated in the UAE and operates under an experimental permit from the Capital Markets Authority in Saudi Arabia, with plans to launch in the Kingdom in Q1 2021.

Budur is an equity crowdfunding platform that connects entrepreneurs, including startups and small and medium enterprises, with a wide pool of investors. This helps overcome challenges entrepreneurs face in securing funding and simplifies complex deal processes. Investors gain access to vetted opportunities, even with small investment amounts, such as 1000 Riyals for Saudi people over 18. Budur charges a 10% success fee on funds raised. The platform aims to support the growth of Saudi Arabia's entrepreneurship ecosystem by fostering transparency and informed decision-making. Budur acquired an experimental CMA fintech license in 2018 and plans to launch its platform in Q1 2021.

## Regulatory Framework and Future Expansion

The Saudi government, through entities like the Saudi Central Bank and the Capital Markets Authority (CMA), provides a supportive regulatory environment for fintech innovation. This includes offering experimental permits, allowing startups like Withhack and Budur to test their solutions in a controlled environment before full market launch. Other companies, such as Falus and Mela, are actively working towards obtaining necessary licenses.

This concerted effort by government efforts, accelerators, and startups aims to solidify Saudi Arabia's position as a regional fintech leader. The focus remains on fostering domestic solutions while also attracting international investment and expertise. The current growth trajectory indicates continued expansion and diversification of financial services within the Kingdom, contributing greatly to its economic transformation goals.
