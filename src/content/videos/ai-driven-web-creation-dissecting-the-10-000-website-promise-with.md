## What Others Missed

While the prospect of $10,000 AI-generated websites is enticing, several critical aspects often go unaddressed in the promotional messaging. Firstly, the "cost" of a website is rarely just the initial code. A truly high-value website includes bespoke branding, complex backend integrations with databases or APIs, unique interactive features, advanced security protocols, ongoing content management, and sophisticated marketing strategies. AI can generate code, but it struggles with deep strategic thinking, genuine creativity for unique brand identity, or the nuanced understanding of a client's specific business processes that often justifies a five-figure price tag.

Secondly, the "AI slop" problem, while potentially mitigated by frameworks like Nano Banana 2, can resurface in subtle ways. Code generated by AI, even when technically functional, may lack the elegance, maintainability, or long-term scalability that a human expert provides. Technical debt can accumulate faster with less optimized AI-generated code. Furthermore, security vulnerabilities might be overlooked by an AI focused on functionality rather than exhaustive threat modeling.

Thirdly, the claim of "SEO ready" needs careful qualification. While AI can structure content and use relevant keywords, comprehensive SEO requires ongoing competitive analysis, technical SEO audits, link building, and sophisticated content strategies that an AI cannot fully automate. The human element of understanding evolving search algorithms and user intent remains paramount. Finally, client communication, revisions, project management, and delivering against subjective aesthetic preferences are inherently human tasks that AI cannot replicate. These soft skills are indispensable for successful high-value web projects.
