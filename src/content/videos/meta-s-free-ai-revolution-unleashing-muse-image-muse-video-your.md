Meta AI, a significant re-entry by Meta into the generative artificial intelligence arena, refers to a new suite of tools for creating visual content. While Meta owns popular communication platforms like WhatsApp, the generative AI capabilities discussed here, specifically for image and video creation, are not currently integrated into WhatsApp. Instead, Meta has launched a free image generation model accessible via meta.ai, with a new video generation tool, Muse Video, on the horizon.

## Meta AI's Image Generation: A "Thinking" Approach

Meta's new image model marks a competitive offering in the generative AI space, positioning itself alongside established tools like GPT image 2 and Nano Banana. Available for free, the model demonstrates a strong ability to interpret and execute prompts, producing detailed images. For instance, when tasked with generating a "man in a blue business suit walking down a busy city street," the model accurately placed the figure on the sidewalk, a notable improvement over earlier AI models that might place characters in the middle of the road. It also handles background elements and text within images with reasonable fidelity, though minor inconsistencies, such as a one-way sign appearing as a two-way sign, can still occur.

A distinguishing feature of Meta's image model is its "thinking" or autoregressive nature. Similar to other advanced models, it can be toggled between "instant" and "thinking" modes, with the latter generally yielding superior results. This "thinking" process allows the AI to iterate internally, refining its understanding and execution of a prompt. Users can even observe this process, seeing how the model considers different elements before settling on a final output. This internal deliberation can sometimes lead to unexpected outcomes, as the model might "talk itself out" of a correct decision it had previously considered.

The model's capabilities are evident in various tests. For complex scene descriptions, like "a glass of wine filled to the top at 3:30 in the afternoon with an analog clock," the model accurately depicts the time on the clock and the wine level, indicating its ability to process multiple, specific instructions. It also shows imagination, as demonstrated by a rendition of "a pelican riding a bike holding a glass of wine," which, despite some surreal elements like a human hand holding the glass, includes thoughtful details such as a satchel on the pelican. Furthermore, it exhibits strong prompt adherence, accurately incorporating elements like white wicker furniture, blue walls, and red carpet from a detailed room description, even uniquely including cassette tapes for the first time in one recurring test.

One area where the model particularly shines is its ability to draw upon specific lore and context. When prompted to create a screenshot of "The Elder Scrolls 6 when it releases in 2031," the model generated an image with accurate in-game elements, including a Level 24 Breton Battlemage in Hammerfell, running at 78 frames a second in 4K. This level of detail and adherence to established lore surpassed other models that might produce generic fantasy scenes. The model also performs well in text generation, creating legible titles and taglines for fictional movie posters.

However, the Meta image model does have some limitations. Characters can sometimes exhibit a "waxy" AI skin texture, and the model struggles with outpainting to different aspect ratios, leading to stretching in some cases. Additionally, when interacting with the model through a chat interface, it can sometimes exhibit "lazy" behavior. For lengthy, multi-part prompts, such as generating storyboards from a detailed beat sheet, the model might initially produce relevant images but then stop generating midway, asking if it should continue. This suggests potential challenges with maintaining context or completing extensive creative tasks without explicit user intervention.

## Muse Video: Meta's Upcoming Generative Video Tool

Beyond image generation, Meta is developing Muse Video, an upcoming generative video model. While still in its early stages, Muse Video promises competitive performance across several key metrics: prompt adherence, visual fidelity, and temporal consistency. Meta is actively investing in improving areas that currently present challenges for generative video, such as audio-to-video synchronization and the accurate depiction of physically fast motion.

Early samples of Muse Video showcase its potential, including short 10-second clips demonstrating multi-shot sequences and natural speech. While it is too early to form a definitive opinion on its overall performance, the samples suggest a promising direction. Initial assessments place Muse Video just below C Dance 2.0 in some industry rankings. Importantly, unlike some of Meta's previous research concepts in video generation, such as Make-A-Video (2022) and MovieGen (2024), which never saw a public release, Muse Video is expected to be made available to users. This commitment indicates Meta's serious intent to establish a presence in the rapidly evolving field of AI video creation.

## The Broader Push for Controllable AI

The developments from Meta are part of a broader industry trend towards more intelligent, controllable, and context-aware AI for visual content creation. The shift is moving beyond simple text-to-image or text-to-video prompts to sophisticated video-to-video manipulation and precise motion control.

Advanced techniques like depth mapping and pose control are becoming increasingly important. These methods allow creators to extract motion and spatial information from existing video footage and then apply it to new generative AI outputs. For example, a tool can track the motion of a character in a source video, generate an OpenPose skeleton (a representation of the character's pose), and map the depth of the scene. This extracted motion and depth data can then serve as a reference for other generative AI models, such as Runway's C-Dance, to create entirely new videos.

This approach enables unprecedented creative flexibility. Users can "re-skin" characters, changing their appearance or even transforming them into entirely different entities, while preserving their original motion. The entire environment can also be swapped out, allowing a character performing an action in one setting to be seamlessly placed into a completely different one, such as a jungle adventurer with a bow and arrow appearing on a derelict spaceship.

However, these advanced techniques still present technical challenges. Running such experiments can be resource-intensive and expensive. Generative models, even with precise control inputs, can sometimes be finicky, occasionally ignoring video inputs or struggling with maintaining consistent context across longer sequences. Despite these hurdles, the ability to precisely control motion, depth, and character pose represents a significant leap forward, promising creators the power to manipulate visual content with a level of detail and specificity previously unattainable. This evolution highlights the industry's drive toward AI tools that not only generate content but also offer deep, intuitive control over the creative process.
