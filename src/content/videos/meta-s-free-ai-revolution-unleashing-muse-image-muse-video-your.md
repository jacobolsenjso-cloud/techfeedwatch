The rapid evolution of generative artificial intelligence continues to reshape how digital content is conceived and produced. Recent strides from major tech players, including Meta, signify a new era of sophisticated tools that push the boundaries of visual creation, moving beyond basic prompt-to-output to nuanced control over complex scenes and motions.

## What It Is

AI image and video generation refers to the process where artificial intelligence algorithms create visual content from text descriptions or existing media inputs. Meta's latest contributions include a new image generation model, accessible via meta.ai, and the forthcoming Muse Video model. These tools are part of a growing class of generative AI designed to produce high-fidelity visuals. Historically, Meta experimented with Make-A-Video in 2022 and the research concept MovieGen in 2024, which aimed for integrated sound. These earlier ventures, while impactful as research, often remained unreleased to the public, setting a precedent that Meta now appears to be changing with its more accessible offerings.

A key innovation in Meta's image model is its categorization as a "thinking" or autoregressive model, similar to advanced counterparts like GPT Image 2 and Nano Banana. Unlike simpler models that generate an image in a single pass, thinking models iteratively refine their output, processing and re-evaluating elements based on the prompt. This iterative approach allows for greater adherence to complex instructions and a more coherent scene composition. Beyond these models, advanced techniques like pose and depth control are transforming video production by enabling precise manipulation of movement and spatial understanding within generated video sequences.

## How It Works

The Meta image model interprets text prompts to construct images through a multi-step process. Users input a description, and the model generates an initial concept, then iteratively refines details, textures, and object placement. Its "show thinking" feature allows users to observe these intermediate steps, revealing how the AI builds its final output. While effective for diverse image generation, the model can sometimes struggle with specific tasks like precise outpainting for varying aspect ratios or maintaining perfect anatomical accuracy, occasionally producing elements like distorted signs or "waxy" character textures.

For video generation, models like the anticipated Muse Video and existing tools like C-Dance or Google's Omni operate on more complex principles. They not only generate visual frames but also ensure temporal consistency, meaning objects and characters move realistically and maintain their identity across the video timeline. When paired with advanced controls, such as the custom tool utilizing depth mapping and OpenPose skeleton tracking, creators can feed existing video footage into the AI. The system extracts the precise pose and depth information of subjects. This data then acts as a scaffold for a new generative process, allowing artists to "re-skin" characters, change environments, or dictate intricate movements while preserving the original motion dynamics. This approach tackles common AI video generation issues like character "popping" in and out of existence or inconsistent actions, delivering a higher degree of control and realism. Concept artists can quickly visualize ideas, while advertising agencies can generate multiple iterations of campaign visuals. Storyboard artists gain immense efficiency in visualizing narrative sequences, especially when coupled with pose and depth control that ensures character consistency across shots. The ability to precisely dictate character actions and environmental layouts makes these tools invaluable for those needing specific visual outcomes.
