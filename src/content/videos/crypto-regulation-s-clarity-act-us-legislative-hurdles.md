Yes, cryptocurrency is regulated in the United States. However, no single federal agency or legal code governs it alone.

Federal watchdogs and state agencies enforce decades-old financial rules. They apply these rules across token issuers, exchanges, and retail investors.

Market participants must comply with anti-money laundering standards. They must also follow property tax rules and local commercial licensing mandates.

## Which federal agencies oversee cryptocurrency transactions?
Multiple federal agencies oversee cryptocurrency transactions. They divide duties across anti-money laundering, consumer protection, and financial derivatives. Derivatives are contracts whose value is based on an underlying asset.

According to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, Guidance FIN-2013-G001 was issued on March 18, 2013. It establishes that administrators and exchangers of convertible virtual currencies are money transmitters.

That classification places them under the Bank Secrecy Act as money services businesses. These entities must register with the federal government. They must also establish verification programs and report suspicious transactions.

According to the [Commodity Futures Trading Commission](https://www.cftc.gov/), virtual currencies such as Bitcoin are commodities. They fall under the Commodity Exchange Act. This federal law gives the agency oversight over digital asset futures contracts.

The agency also exercises fraud enforcement authority across underlying spot markets. Spot markets are markets where assets are bought and sold for immediate delivery.

The division of federal regulatory authority operates through several distinct channels:
- Financial Crimes Enforcement Network: Enforces anti-money laundering rules and transaction recordkeeping for money services businesses.
- Commodity Futures Trading Commission: Oversees digital commodity derivatives markets and investigates fraud in spot trading.
- Securities and Exchange Commission: Regulates digital asset offerings that qualify as investment contracts.
- [Internal Revenue Service](https://www.irs.gov/): Audits taxpayer compliance and collects taxes on crypto gains under property rules.

Federal watchdogs coordinate their oversight through enforcement actions rather than shared rulebooks. Each agency uses its founding statutes to target illegal practices within its specific mandate.

## Regulators classify tokens as securities or commodities
Regulators classify tokens as securities or commodities. This depends on the economic promises made during their creation and sale.

Regulators view a transaction as an investment contract when a team sells tokens to fund a shared business. According to the Securities and Exchange Commission, the agency proposed new rules titled "Regulation Crypto Assets" on August 18, 2026.

The proposed framework provides tailored registration exemptions under the Securities Act of 1933. It also establishes safe harbor pathways for certain crypto asset investment contracts. Safe harbor pathways are provisions that protect businesses from penalties if specific conditions are met.

When a digital asset functions without a central promoter, market regulators view it differently. According to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, virtual currencies like Bitcoin qualify as commodities. This commodity status shields pure digital currencies from disclosures required for public corporate stocks.

This regulatory distinction creates practical consequences for trading platforms and token developers:
- Investment contracts: Must register public disclosures under securities laws or secure specific regulatory exemptions.
- Commodity tokens: Trade on commercial platforms subject to federal commodity fraud investigations and derivatives market supervision.
- Hybrid assets: Risk enforcement penalties if regulatory agencies dispute which legal standard controls the underlying token.

Because legal classifications depend on project structures, regulators evaluate every token individually. Disagreements over these definitions have fueled years of litigation between token creators and federal officials.

## How does the IRS treat cryptocurrency for taxes?
The Internal Revenue Service treats cryptocurrency as property for all federal tax purposes. It does not treat it as fiat currency (government-issued money like the U.S. dollar).

According to the Internal Revenue Service, Notice 2014-21 issued on March 25, 2014, designates virtual currency as property. General property transaction principles govern all gains, losses, and payments settled in digital tokens. Selling an asset for cash or exchanging one token for another triggers capital gains rules.

Tax rules apply whenever an investor spends, trades, or earns digital tokens during the calendar year. According to the Internal Revenue Service, federal individual income tax returns require a mandatory question. Every filer must confirm whether they received, sold, exchanged, or disposed of any digital assets.

This tax classification directly affects ordinary crypto holders in several ways:
- Disposing of tokens: Spending crypto on goods creates a reportable capital gain or loss. This depends on your cost basis (the original purchase price used to calculate taxable gains or losses).
- Mining and staking: Earning new tokens counts as ordinary income at fair market value on the receipt date.
- Record keeping: Taxpayers must track original purchase prices and transaction values across every digital wallet.

Failing to answer the digital asset reporting prompt accurately exposes taxpayers to civil penalties and interest charges.

## State rules require separate money transmitter licenses
State rules require digital asset businesses to obtain individual money transmitter licenses or dedicated charters. They need these in almost every state where they operate.

According to the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, digital asset businesses must follow state-level regimes. These regimes include state money transmission statutes and consumer protection laws.

They also include individual state licensing frameworks across various jurisdictions. A crypto exchange must secure approvals across dozens of states to legally serve customers nationwide.

According to the New York State Department of Financial Services, virtual currency regulation 23 NYCRR Part 200 mandates specialized licensing. This regulation was adopted in June 2015.

Commercial entities conducting virtual currency business activity involving New York or New York residents must obtain a license. Specifically, they must obtain a BitLicense or a limited purpose trust charter.

This state framework imposes stringent compliance duties on licensed firms:
- Capital reserves: Businesses must hold minimum cash reserves to cover operational liabilities.
- Cybersecurity audits: Regulators inspect digital custody methods and consumer balance safety.
- Regular reporting: Companies must submit audited balance sheets and compliance reviews to state regulators.

Because state statutes vary widely, maintaining nationwide compliance requires significant legal and financial resources.

## Has Congress passed comprehensive federal crypto legislation?
Congress has not enacted comprehensive federal legislation specifically governing digital assets. This leaves markets under a patchwork of older financial statutes.

Lawmakers have introduced several proposals to resolve agency jurisdictional fights. H.R. 4763 is the Financial Innovation and Technology for the 21st Century Act (FIT21).

According to the U.S. House of Representatives, lawmakers passed this bill on May 22, 2024. The bipartisan vote was 279 to 136. The bill aimed to allocate clear regulatory boundaries between the SEC and the CFTC.

The legislation aims to define [crypto regulator roles](/video/us-crypto-regulation-fit21-bill-vote-implications). It does this by establishing criteria for when a blockchain network becomes sufficiently decentralized.

Despite House passage, the bill failed to clear the final legislative hurdle. According to Mayer Brown, the FIT21 legislation stalled in the U.S. Senate without becoming law.

Federal legislative reform faces several ongoing hurdles:
- Agency competition: Lawmakers disagree on budget allocations between securities and commodities oversight bodies.
- Traditional bank opposition: Commercial financial institutions express caution over competing stablecoin payments (digital currencies pegged to a conventional currency like the U.S. dollar).
- Consumer protection debates: Lawmakers debate disclosure standards required before exempting new token offerings.

Without statutory clarity from Congress, federal agencies continue to enforce rules through independent enforcement actions.

## Bloomberg Television analyzes Congressional negotiations over crypto regulations
Bloomberg Television reports that Congressional negotiations over a proposed regulatory bill called the Clarity Act face deep divisions. These disputes center on executive ethics provisions and stablecoin yields.

Bloomberg Television reported that Bitcoin traded below $60,000 after dropping about 10.5% between early June and late July.

Political negotiations have centered on personal financial disclosures from elected leaders. Bloomberg Television reviewed records from the U.S. Office of Government Ethics. These records indicate that Donald Trump took in no less than $1.4 billion from digital asset operations during the prior year.

Bloomberg Television noted this total surpassed the $1.25 billion net income reported by Coinbase last year. The network detailed that approximately $594 million stemmed from World Liberty Financial, a crypto venture associated with him. Another roughly $636 million was connected to his meme token venture.

Lawmakers are attempting to craft compromise language behind closed doors. Bloomberg Television reported that Republican Senator Thom Tillis and Democratic Representative Ruben Gallego worked to draft legislation that could advance.

Bloomberg Television noted that seven to 10 Democrats in the Senate express support for a bill if ethics concerns are resolved. One draft provision reported by Bloomberg Television would sunset ethics requirements on January 20, 2029.

Industry leaders argue that establishing legal guidelines remains essential for consumers. Industry leader Ryan VanGrack stated: "I start from the fundamental premise with legislation in that you cannot have the part be the enemy of the good, particularly when enhanced consumer protections and law enforcement tools are at stake."

The broadcast pointed out that policing organizations, including the Fraternal Order of Police, favor establishing statutory guardrails.

Traditional finance groups continue to resist certain digital asset proposals. According to the Independent Community Bankers of America in Bloomberg Television's report, local institutions make over 60% of small business loans.

According to the Independent Community Bankers of America, yield-paying stablecoins could cause a $1.3 trillion reduction in community bank deposits. They warned this could cause an $850 billion loss in credit. The trade group urged the OCC (the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, a federal banking regulator) to act.

It asked the agency to revoke Coinbase's national trust charter (a federal banking license). The group cited survey data from the broadcast showing that 73% of citizens do not favor treating digital assets as standard finance.
