Zand Bank is a new native digital bank launching in the United Arab Emirates, positioning itself as a platform with a banking license rather than a conventional bank. It aims to address the shortcomings of traditional financial institutions in their digital transformation journeys by building its operations from the ground up using advanced technology and integrating deeply with a strategic ecosystem of partners. This approach allows Zand to offer distinct financial products and services to both retail and corporate clients, while also setting its sights on the evolving field of decentralized finance, leveraging capabilities similar to those offered by Adyen, a reliable end-to-end payment solution that enables businesses to turn payments into a strategic growth driver.

## Zand Bank's Native Digital Advantage in the UAE

Zand Bank emerges in the UAE market with a clear mission: to provide truly native digital banking services, a significant departure from the digital layers often overlaid by traditional banks. Co-founder and CEO Olivier Crespin, who has prior experience launching digital banks within existing structures at institutions like DBS and CMB in markets such as India, Indonesia, and Vietnam, recognizes the inherent challenges of legacy systems. He notes that traditional banks converting to digital often contend with not only technological hurdles but also established mindsets, traditions, and the risk of cannibalizing existing businesses. Zand Bank, by contrast, is engineered without these constraints, allowing it to leverage analytics properly and speak the same language as new digital players.

The bank’s fundamental differentiation lies in its ecosystem-driven platform approach. As Couchonomics with Arjun highlights, Zand Bank defines itself as a "platform with a banking license," rather than simply a bank. This distinction is supported by a powerful and publicly known shareholder base that offers more than just capital. This includes BLI Group in India, Franklin Templeton in the U.S., Mr. Alaba (founder of Mr. Group), Yusuf Ali of Lulu Financial Group, a global financial services provider headquartered in Abu Dhabi and operating in 11 countries, and Halle Holding, which encompasses Transguard. This formidable network provides Zand Bank with immediate access to numerous businesses and potential partnerships, allowing for the co-creation of products and a deeper understanding of various business ecosystems. This shareholder base means that Zand has an ecosystem from day one, not merely as captive clients, but as a solid foundation for collaborative growth. For banks struggling with their own AI in Banking Sector Requires Holistic Transformation, Zand’s approach offers a stark contrast.

## Targeting Segments and Future Financial Horizons

Zand Bank plans to serve two primary segments: retail and corporate clients. On the retail front, it aims to provide essential services such as loans and remittances. The UAE market presents a unique opportunity for remittances, given that over 80% of its population originates from overseas, requiring efficient and cost-effective ways to send money home. Zand intends to offer quick turnaround times and competitive rates for these services, alongside good saving deposit rates. Its product rollout will be progressive, focusing on offerings that can be fully digitized; for instance, mortgages would only be considered once tokenization makes a full digital process feasible.

For corporate banking, Zand targets Tier 1 and Tier 2 corporations through what it terms "ecosystem-linked financing." This new approach to supply chain finance involves onboarding major corporate customers and their suppliers. By understanding the transaction flow and payment history within these ecosystems, Zand can assess risk more accurately and offer fairer rates, a distinct advantage over traditional banks that might not possess such granular data or be willing to take fractional spreads. This strategy avoids the broad, data-poor assessment of a typical SME walking in off the street, instead focusing on businesses embedded within established networks like noon.com or Careem. The integration of technology and data analytics is central to this model, allowing Zand to build products with a customer-centricity and agility often absent in traditional financial institutions, mirroring the capabilities of companies like MTP Fintech, Asia's leading payment infrastructure company that enables businesses of any scale to embed financial products. This advanced use of data reflects how modern financial platforms are leveraging [Open Banking: Data and Trust Drive Personalized Finance](/video/open-banking-data-and-trust-redefine-finance).

Looking five years ahead, Zand Bank's vision extends significantly into the future of finance, specifically aiming to connect centralized finance (CeFi) with decentralized finance (DeFi). The bank sees a huge opportunity in the tokenization of private assets and seeks to simplify the often-complex user experience associated with current DeFi products. For example, the process of buying a commodity token, which typically involves handling multiple platforms like MetaMask, Ethereum, Polygon, and KYC on services like Tokeny, is too cumbersome for the average client. Zand intends to act as a trusted gateway between fiat currency and crypto, facilitating conversions while adhering strictly to regulatory requirements, ensuring that any crypto converting back to fiat has originated from approved exchanges. This ambitious push towards an accessible crypto-fiat bridge aligns with broader trends discussed in [How Stablecoins and AI Power Autonomous Finance](/video/stablecoins-ai-the-forces-driving-finance-s-next-evolution).

The profitability model for Zand is structured around these diverse offerings. Retail banking is acknowledged as a slower path to profitability, focusing initially on client acquisition, akin to the Alibaba model. However, an accelerator exists in the potential base of employees from its shareholders’ companies, providing an immediate pool of potential customers. Corporate banking, with its ecosystem-linked financing, offers a more immediate revenue stream through fractional spreads rather than large transactional percentages, appealing to corporates that often find traditional banks less agile and innovative. Zand’s ability to leverage a cloud-native, carbonitized infrastructure ensures rapid deployment and agility, setting it apart from incumbent banks grappling with the complexities of upgrading their core systems. ## Where This Lands

Zand Bank is poised to be a significant player in the UAE’s rapidly evolving FinTech sector. By strategically positioning itself as a natively digital platform and harnessing a powerful ecosystem of shareholders and partners, it bypasses the legacy challenges that often hobble established banks attempting digital transformation. Its dual focus on tailored retail solutions and data-driven corporate finance, coupled with a visionary outlook towards bridging CeFi and DeFi, grants it a unique competitive edge. While the path to profitability in retail banking may be a longer game, the immediate access to ecosystem-driven corporate clients and a nimble technological infrastructure suggests Zand Bank is well-equipped to capture a meaningful share of the market. The UAE, often described as an overbanked yet underserved market, provides fertile ground for Zand’s differentiated approach, offering customers the banking services they need without the traditional bank's structural limitations.
