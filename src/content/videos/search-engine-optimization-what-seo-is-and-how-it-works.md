Search Engine Optimization (SEO) involves strategies to enhance a website's visibility within search engine results, primarily Google, to attract more qualified traffic. It works by aligning a website's content and technical structure with the algorithms search engines use to rank information, ultimately aiming to deliver the most relevant answers to user queries.

Google, which is the dominant search engine in markets like Australia, operates with a vision "to make the world's information universally accessible to everyone." Its product, Google search (found at google.com.au), strives to "allow people to reach their destination, whatever they're searching for as quickly as possible." This fundamental goal means Google actually "don't want people to spend too much time on their Google search product, Google.com. They don't want it to move off." To achieve this, Google employs a complex, proprietary algorithm that it does not disclose to anyone. An SEO consultant emphasizes a "red flag" if "anyone says that they know the Google algorithm, the SEO algorithm," noting that professionals "through experimentation, through testing, and through tidbits, will know part of it or a lot of it, but never all of it." The underlying principle, akin to the secret recipe of Coke, is that "the aim of the game is to be relevant."

Effective SEO centers on two key practices. The first, as Peppermint iT Sip ‘n Solve explains, is keyword research. As Louise pointed out, "You just explained the very first step of SEO, Louise, which is what we call keyword research." This initial step involves identifying the exact words and phrases potential customers use when searching for products or services. These "keyword examples" can range from "computer problem" and "computer help" to "managed services," "new computers," or "security." Keyword research is "the first critical part of the project where you discover find out where all the popularity in terms of the terms are." The second critical practice is, as the source states, "to make sure that your website has those keywords on it." Consider Google's perspective: if a user searches for "managed IT services," Google would prioritize a website with "10 mentions of managed IT services" over one with "zero mentions." If a website has zero mentions, "Because they're not relevant. Because if the user hits that website, they don't see anything that's related to them. What are they going to do? They're gonna bounce." This outcome is "not good for Google because users would think that Google has sent them to a poor quality website." This active incorporation of keywords and ensuring content relevance directly impacts visibility and [SEO Optimization Today: How Modern Search Visibility Really Works](/video/seo-optimization-explained-how-modern-search-ranking-functions).

## The Misconception and Cost of Neglect
A common pitfall arises when SEO is treated as an afterthought. Many businesses approach web designers who often "focus on the aesthetics" and elements like user interface (UI) and user experience (UX). While UI and UX are vital for engaging visitors, they are distinct from the specific technical and content optimizations that drive search engine visibility. This often results in SEO becoming a "second stage, third stage afterthought" once a website is already built. This delay means "you're doubling up on the work" later, incurring additional costs and effort to restructure or rewrite content to achieve proper ranking. [What User Experience Means for Modern SEO Rankings](/video/frontend-seo-s-technical-core-why-user-experience-now-dominates) highlights the importance of user experience, but it must work in tandem with search engine relevance.

## The Bottom Line
Ultimately, SEO is not merely a technical exercise but a strategic imperative. It requires understanding Google's core mission to deliver the most relevant information quickly, and then building a website that genuinely meets that criterion. Proactive integration of keyword research and content relevance from the outset, rather than after design is complete, ensures a website is not only visually appealing but also discoverable and effective in reaching its target audience. As such, "SEO definitely should be always thought of in parallel and before to any type of website build. Absolutely." This approach is critical for sustained online success and avoiding costly retrofitting.
