Authentication is the process of verifying a user's identity, confirming they are who they claim to be. Authenticity, on the other hand, refers to the quality of being genuine or real. In digital banking, the focus is on strong authentication to ensure that only legitimate users access their accounts and services, moving beyond simple identity checks to confirm the genuine nature of the user's interaction. This shift is vital for securing financial transactions and fostering trust in online platforms.

## The Evolution of Digital Identity Verification

The way we prove who we are has changed dramatically over time. Historically, physical methods like showing a credit card with a name and signature were common. A shopkeeper could visually compare the cardholder to the name on the card. As financial interactions moved online, replicating this physical check became a large challenge.

Early attempts at digital identity verification often involved digitizing paper processes. This meant asking users to physically visit a branch, or later, to upload photos of identification documents and record selfie videos. These methods were essentially digital versions of a teller seeing and recognizing a customer. While they offered a basic level of security, they were often cumbersome, prone to human error, and did not fully address the unique challenges of the online environment where a person cannot be physically seen. The goal was to recreate the physical check digitally, but the available technology was limited.

## Behavioral Biometrics: A New Approach to Authentication

Modern digital identity solutions, particularly those using artificial intelligence (AI), move beyond simply digitizing physical checks. They focus on identifying users purely digitally, without relying on physical biometrics like fingerprints or facial scans, or traditional knowledge-based authentication like passwords. This is achieved through behavioral biometrics.

Behavioral biometrics analyzes how a user interacts with their device and applications. This includes patterns in typing speed, mouse movements, how they hold their phone, or even the way they navigate through an app. AI models continuously learn and adapt to a user's unique digital behavior. When a user attempts to log in or make a transaction, the system compares their current behavior against their established profile. If the behavior deviates much, it can flag the activity as suspicious, even if traditional credentials like a password are correct. This approach shifts the focus from merely filtering out bad actors to positively identifying legitimate users based on their unique digital footprint.

## Operational Efficiency and Security: The OakNorth Example

The success of digital banks like OakNorth highlights how meticulous operational efficiency and a deep understanding of customer segments contribute to profitability. OakNorth, for instance, focused on serving growth entrepreneurs, a segment often underserved by traditional banks and therefore incorrectly priced. Their strategy wasn't just about offering a "cool product" but about building a fundamentally sound and profitable banking business.

A key aspect of their operational efficiency involved minimizing costs across the board, including those related to fraud and customer support. For example, OakNorth chose to raise deposits through pure online savings accounts rather than current accounts. While savings accounts might pay a 1% interest rate, they found that the overall cost of funding was lower. Current accounts, despite often having 0% interest, incurred a "half a percent" higher overall cost due to extensive anti-money laundering (AML) and client due diligence (CDD) requirements, and the need to screen every transaction. Savings accounts, with their fixed terms and less frequent activity, required a much smaller call center and support staff, much shrinking operational expenses. AI authentication and advanced digital identity solutions directly support this model by reducing fraud rates and the operational burden of verifying identities, leading to higher margins and greater profitability.

OakNorth's internal platform for screening and monitoring borrowers was so effective that it was spun off into OakNorth Technologies. This platform is now used by "more than a dozen, if nearly 20" other banks outside the UK, demonstrating the value of strong, data-driven approaches to risk assessment and identity management in lending.

## Benefits and Challenges of AI Authentication

AI authentication offers large benefits for digital banking. It enhances security by providing a continuous, real-time layer of protection that adapts to user behavior. This makes it much harder for fraudsters to impersonate legitimate users, even if they obtain credentials. For genuine customers, it creates a lower-friction experience, as they may not need to remember complex passwords or undergo frequent manual verification steps. This improved user experience can lead to higher customer satisfaction and engagement. And, by reducing fraud and the need for extensive manual reviews, AI authentication can much lower operational costs for banks.

However, implementing AI authentication also presents challenges. Privacy concerns are paramount, as these systems collect and analyze sensitive behavioral data. Banks must ensure transparency and strict adherence to data protection regulations. There is also the potential for false positives, where a legitimate user is incorrectly identified as a fraudster, or false negatives, where a fraudster slips through. AI models require continuous training and adaptation to new fraud patterns and evolving user behaviors. Initial how it works can also involve large investment in technology and expertise.

## Implementing Advanced Digital Identity Solutions

The journey to advanced digital identity solutions began with companies like Callsign, founded in 2012. The first "5 years" involved extensive research by PhDs, translating theoretical concepts into practical products. It has only been in the last "4 or 5 years now" that the technology has become widely available and evolved to truly identify users purely digitally.

For banks, implementing these solutions means moving away from simply digitizing old processes. It involves adopting systems that can analyze complex data patterns in real-time to build a dynamic profile of each user. This includes integrating AI-powered tools that monitor user interactions, device characteristics, and network information. The goal is to create a multi-layered security approach where authentication is not a single event but an ongoing process, constantly verifying the authenticity of the user and their actions. This continuous verification helps banks maintain high security standards while providing a smooth, efficient experience for their customers.
