An iPhone takes approximately 30 minutes to reach a 50 percent charge when using an 18W or higher USB-C adapter, according to Apple Support.

Older low-wattage bricks and uncertified wireless pads take substantially longer.

## How fast do different chargers fill an iPhone?

Charging times differ significantly across wired fast chargers, legacy 5W adapters, and wireless chargers. Apple Support states that an Apple 18W or higher USB-C power adapter provides up to 50 percent charge in approximately 30 minutes on supported devices, and comparable third-party USB Power Delivery adapters achieve the same speed.

According to Apple Support, charging an iPhone might take longer than you expect if you use a wired charger that provides 7.5W or less, such as a legacy 5W adapter.

Wireless chargers also vary substantially by hardware type. Apple Support confirms that wireless fast charging using a MagSafe Charger paired with a 30W or higher power adapter can charge iPhone 16 and iPhone 17 handsets to 50 percent in around 30 minutes.

Conversely, standard Qi1 (an older wireless charging standard) wireless charging pads supplying under 10W take significantly longer, according to [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102574).

The table below contrasts real-world charging benchmarks and power levels recorded by Apple Support, GSMArena, and TechRadar.

| Charger and Device Setup | Speed to 50 Percent | Speed to 100 Percent | Peak Power Recorded |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Apple 18W+ USB-C Adapter (Apple Support) | ~30 minutes | Not specified | 18W or higher |
| MagSafe with 30W Adapter (Apple Support) | ~30 minutes | Not specified | Not specified |
| iPhone 16 Pro with Fast USB-PD (GSMArena) | Not specified | 1 hour and 38 minutes | ~30W |
| iPhone 16 Pro Max with 45W Charger (GSMArena) | 46% at 30 minutes | Not specified | ~30W |
| Legacy 7.5W or lower USB Brick (Apple Support) | Substantially longer | Substantially longer | 7.5W or less |

Connecting a massive laptop charger will not speed up the process indefinitely. Tests by ChargerLAB reported by TechRadar showed that an Apple iPhone 16 Pro Max peaked at 30.15 watts on an Apple 70W brick.

TechRadar reported that the same phone drew 28.39 watts on an Apple 30W charger. The phone hardware sets a strict ceiling on incoming wattage.

## Charging slows past 80% to prevent battery degradation

According to Apple Support, charging slows down after 80 percent because iOS reduces incoming current to protect lithium-ion chemical cells from excessive heat and stress.

Lithium-ion batteries accept power rapidly when empty. They build internal resistance as they fill up.

Pumping high wattage into an almost full cell generates intense heat. That heat degrades battery capacity over time.

[DXOMARK](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-15-pro-battery-test/) reported that an Apple iPhone 15 Pro required 54 minutes to reach 80 percent charge. That same phone required 1 hour and 56 minutes to reach 100 percent, according to DXOMARK. The final 20 percent took longer than the first 80 percent combined.

In lab tests by GSMArena, an Apple iPhone 16 Pro took 1 hour and 38 minutes to reach a full charge. GSMArena reported that the final 15 percent alone required about 40 minutes due to aggressive power tapering.

Software settings also intervene intentionally. Apple Support explains that Optimized Battery Charging learns daily habits and delays charging past 80 percent during long connections.

The feature finishes the final 20 percent shortly before you unplug. This software control minimizes cell stress and extends battery lifespan.

## What factors slow down your iPhone charging speed?

Thermal conditions, power source wattage, active phone use, and background software tasks dictate how fast your iPhone charges.

Extreme ambient temperatures disrupt charging performance immediately. Apple Support warns that fast charging fails to operate properly in rooms that are either too hot or too cold. When an [iPhone gets hot](/video/why-is-my-iphone-hot-and-how-ios-regulates-heat), safety firmware throttles incoming current to prevent hardware damage.

Hardware accessories create additional bottlenecks:
- Low-output power bricks supplying 7.5W or less take longer to charge an iPhone, according to Apple Support.
- Standard Qi1 wireless pads delivering under 10W take longer to charge an iPhone, according to Apple Support.
- High-wattage chargers above 30W yield diminishing returns because an iPhone 16 Pro Max peaks at approximately 30W, according to GSMArena.
- Heavy gaming or continuous screen brightness while plugged in diverts incoming electricity away from the battery.

In practice, this means you should not panic if your phone charges slowly in a warm car or on a cheap desk pad. A cool room and a certified USB Power Delivery plug remain the most reliable combination.

## Track charging progress and slow charger alerts in iOS

You will not find an on-screen countdown timer in iOS. Instead, Apple Support states that iOS shows a Slow Charger alert on the Lock Screen and inside Battery Settings when an adapter supplies lower wattage than recommended for faster charging.

You can track charging progress and estimate completion time using standard iOS tools:
- Estimate remaining time against known baselines: Apple Support notes compatible iPhones hit 50 percent in roughly 30 minutes on an 18W or higher USB-C plug, or about 30 minutes via MagSafe with a 30W adapter on iPhone 16 and 17 handsets. Full charges take 1 hour 38 minutes in GSMArena tests, or 1 hour 56 minutes in DXOMARK tests.
- Check Battery under Settings to view real-time charge graphs and recent power use.
- Look for the Slow Charger label inside Battery Settings if your phone seems sluggish on the wire, as outlined by Apple Support.
- Adjust Optimized Battery Charging in Settings to match your personal routine.
- Switch on Airplane Mode or leave the screen off to stop background power drain during quick pit stops.

These tools help you spot weak cables or underpowered hubs before heading out with a low battery.

## Cameron Dougherty Tech tests iPhone 18 Pro 40W charging

[Cameron Dougherty Tech](https://bio.site/camerondoughertytech) tested an empty iPhone 18 Pro using an Apple 40W Dynamic Power Adapter to see whether the device reaches a 50 percent charge in 15 minutes.

The test paired the phone with the stock Apple cable, a Power-Z USB tester, and a thermal camera. Cameron Dougherty Tech noted that the Apple 40W Dynamic Power Adapter costs $39, making it pricier than third-party options from Anker and UGREEN.

The adapter relies on adjustable voltage supply (AVS, a feature that fine-tunes electrical voltage to optimize charging), which Cameron Dougherty Tech described as "the secret sauce behind the scenes that enables these super fast results."

Power delivery ramped up quickly during the test. Cameron Dougherty Tech recorded 6% battery and a charge rate just over 40 watts after 2 minutes.

The tester expected power delivery boosting closer to 60 watts, but the Power-Z meter indicated a PD3.0 connection (an older USB Power Delivery standard) instead of Power Delivery 3.2 (a newer fast-charging protocol). Battery levels climbed steadily, hitting 10% at minute three, 13% at minute four, and 17% at minute five.

The thermal camera showed that phone temperatures stayed under 90° during early stages, while the charger hovered slightly below that mark. Cameron Dougherty Tech recorded the battery hitting 21%, then 24%, 28%, and 31% over subsequent check-ins. By minute 10, the phone reached 33% battery while the charger warmed to 95°.

The battery hit 47% at 15 minutes. Cameron Dougherty Tech recorded the battery officially crossing 50% at 16 minutes and 12 seconds.

While the phone missed the strict 15-minute mark, Cameron Dougherty Tech stated that Apple claims "around 15 minutes" rather than an exact cutoff.
