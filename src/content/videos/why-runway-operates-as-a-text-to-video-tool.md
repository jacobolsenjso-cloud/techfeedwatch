Runway is a text-to-video tool that generates synthetic video clips directly from written descriptions. According to Runway platform documentation, the software creates dynamic visual scenes from text prompts, uploaded still images, and pre-existing video footage. Creators use the system to build original media sequences without physical studio equipment.

## Which Runway models support text-to-video generation?
Runway supports text-to-video generation primarily through its flagship Gen-4.5 architecture alongside a broad library of specialized rendering engines. According to Runway Research, the company launched its first publicly accessible text-to-video workflow with the Gen-2 model in early 2023.

Subsequent updates transitioned the platform toward faster and more detailed outputs. According to Runway Help Center documentation, the company retired its Gen-3 Alpha model on July 8, 2026, followed by Gen-3 Alpha Turbo on July 30, 2026. Those retirements established Gen-4.5 as the core engine for production workflows across the web platform.

For technical teams integrating video pipelines directly into external software, developer access extends beyond the primary consumer interface:

- Runway developer documentation confirms API access to 22 text-to-video models.
- The same developer documentation lists 23 image-to-video models available via API endpoints.
- Developer documentation also details 11 video-to-video models tailored for motion restyling.

According to New York University's Tisch School of the Arts, NYU alumni Cristóbal Valenzuela, Anastasis Germanidis, and Alejandro Matamala founded Runway in 2018. Upstarts Media reports that the company has secured more than $540 million in private investment and achieved a corporate valuation of $5.3 billion.

## Runway provides multimodal generation beyond simple text prompts
Runway provides multimodal generation by accepting text descriptions, static images, and reference video clips as foundational generation inputs. Users do not depend entirely on written prompts to define physical motion or camera behavior. 

Modern generative workflows let operators establish lighting, camera angles, and character features through mixed media sources. Studios regularly evaluate which tool is really the [best AI video generator](/video/which-tool-is-really-the-best-ai-video-generator) to handle these hybrid technical requirements. Runway addresses this demand by pairing natural language prompting with precise spatial controls.

Creators deploy several input methods when setting up a production asset on the platform:

- Direct text descriptions to establish narrative themes and broad worldbuilding.
- Static photograph inputs that set exact costume details, facial structure, and lighting conditions.
- Pre-existing video reference clips that map movement dynamics and camera trajectories.

## How does Runway text-to-video compare to image-to-video?
Text-to-video excels at open-ended brainstorming, whereas image-to-video delivers precise framing, strict spatial control, and subject consistency. Choosing between the two options depends on the level of predictability a project requires.

According to Runway Resources, the platform advises operators to rely on text-to-video for fast concept exploration and surreal visual sequences. When a project requires exact actor appearances or specific product geometry, text alone often introduces unwanted variations across frames.

Runway Resources notes that supplying an initial reference image gives directors significantly greater command over scene composition and character continuity. Creators frequently generate a static concept image first and then animate that frame to prevent anatomical warping.

## Gen-4.5 achieves photorealism with distinct subject limitations
Gen-4.5 generates near-photorealistic environment footage, yet it still struggles to render complex human features with complete fidelity.

According to a Runway Research paper titled "The Turing Reel" published in January 2026, 1,043 human participants achieved an overall detection accuracy of only 57.1% when trying to distinguish five-second Gen-4.5 clips from authentic video. This result demonstrates that viewers mistake generated sequences for camera capture almost half the time.

According to Runway Research findings from January 2026:

- Human detection accuracy reached 58% to 65% when evaluators inspected human faces and hands.
- According to Runway Research, detection accuracy dropped below random chance to 45% to 47% on architectural scenes and animal footage.

Structural textures like concrete, foliage, and fur mask algorithmic artifacts effectively. Human anatomy remains harder for neural networks to reproduce without subtle motion errors.
