You can make AI videos right now using web browsers, generative text prompts, and reference images. Modern video models transform descriptive text and still photographs into short motion sequences within minutes. Producing a finished video project requires stitching those short generations together in a standard editing timeline with audio, sound effects, and transitions.

## What tools can you use to generate AI videos?
Creators generate AI videos using cloud-based platforms that turn text scripts, still images, and keyframe prompts into moving visual clips.

Some focus on photorealistic cinematic action, while others specialize in virtual workplace presentations.

- Synthetic presenter platforms: According to [Synthesia](https://www.synthesia.io/), creators produce talking-head videos directly in a web browser from written scripts across more than 140 languages using synthetic avatars without physical cameras or studio sets.
- Design-integrated animators: According to Canva, its video generator turns text prompts into short clips with synchronized audio and animates static portrait photos into talking presenters across 40 languages.
- Cinematic camera engines: According to Luma AI, its platform generates video clips from text prompts or still images while providing camera motion controls and start-and-end frame keyframing.
- Multimodal foundation models: According to Google Cloud Documentation, the Veo 3.1 model handles text-to-video, image-to-video, and first-to-last-frame generation.

Finding the [easiest way to make AI videos](/video/what-is-the-easiest-way-to-make-ai-videos) often comes down to choosing between an all-in-one web editor and a dedicated prompt-to-video generation engine.

## Most AI video models generate short clips, not full movies
Current AI video systems generate short visual bursts, typically between about 5 and 20 seconds per generation, rather than continuous full-length movies.

According to Google Cloud Documentation, the Veo 3.1 model creates clips in specific lengths of 4, 6, or 8 seconds at 24 frames per second, exporting outputs in 720p, 1080p, or 4K resolution. These short durations stem from the extreme computing power required to render consistent visual frames. Longer outputs frequently suffer from physical distortions, warping hands, and shifting backgrounds.

Rendering motion alongside audio creates additional technical demands. According to Google Cloud, the Veo 3.1 model utilizes a joint diffusion process that generates video visuals and audio tracks simultaneously. This process synchronizes ambient sound, special effects, and character dialogue directly with on-screen movement.

Because single generations stop after a few seconds, narrative storytelling requires building a multi-clip pipeline. Creators generate scenes shot by shot, maintaining visual continuity through reference photos. Once all individual clips render, an editor cuts them together into a coherent sequence.

## How much does AI video generation cost?
AI video generation costs range from free promotional trials with resolution limits to recurring monthly subscriptions starting around twelve dollars.

Generative video relies on expensive graphics hardware in data centers, prompting platforms to use credit-based pricing models. Free plans offer a small allocation of credits to test rendering quality, while production-grade resolutions require ongoing paid plans.

- Free trial tiers: According to Runway's official pricing documentation, the service offers a Free plan with 125 one-time credits that produces watermarked exports.
- Standard subscriptions: Runway's pricing documentation confirms paid plans start at $12 per month billed annually, or $15 month-to-month, providing 625 monthly credits and watermark-free downloads.
- Enterprise compute platforms: Cloud platforms like Google Cloud charge commercial developers per second of rendered compute for models like Veo 3.1.

Platform availability also shifts quickly as companies adjust their product lines. According to OpenAI's Help Center, the Sora web and mobile applications were discontinued on April 26, 2026, and the Sora API was discontinued on September 24, 2026. Creators must monitor platform roadmaps so active production workflows do not disappear abruptly.

## Copyright protects human editing rather than raw AI prompts
Whether creators can legally own, copyright, and monetize AI-generated videos depends on the level of human creative authorship involved. Raw video generated directly from text prompts cannot receive copyright protection, but human editors can protect fully assembled productions.

According to the U.S. Copyright Office's Part 2 Report on Copyright and Artificial Intelligence published on January 29, 2025, outputs generated solely by AI in response to user prompts cannot receive copyright protection because prompts do not exercise sufficient human creative control over the final expressive elements. Simply typing a descriptive prompt into an engine leaves the expressive heavy lifting to the software.

According to the U.S. Copyright Office's Part 2 Report published on January 29, 2025, human creators can copyright AI-assisted video works if human authors contribute perceptible expressive inputs or execute creative selection, arrangement, and editing of the material. Putting clips into a specific order, cutting scenes to a beat, and layering custom audio adds legally recognized human authorship.

Formal registration requires open disclosure. According to the U.S. Copyright Office's March 2023 Registration Guidance, applicants seeking to register copyright for works containing AI-generated elements must explicitly disclose and disclaim any AI-generated content exceeding de minimis amounts as unclaimable material.

## Ai Lockup's Zero-Dollar Workflow for Multi-Shot AI Films
Creator Ai Lockup outlines a three-step production workflow that produces structured, multi-scene narrative videos without paying subscription fees.

The production sequence starts by asking ChatGPT to act as a film director, dividing a story concept into individual scenes with dedicated image and motion prompts. Ai Lockup explains that this production framework easily expands to create projects lasting 5 minutes, 10 minutes, or extended durations.

Solving visual inconsistency represents the core technical challenge in multi-shot generation. As Ai Lockup points out, "the character's face changes in every shot" when beginners enter raw prompts into an engine without reference data.

Ai Lockup resolves this problem in Flow AI, a web tool that provides 50 free credits daily. The creator generates a character reference sheet, locks the facial features under a character profile named Father, and links those visual traits across all subsequent scene generations.

Ai Lockup sets the canvas to a 16 to 9 aspect ratio and renders initial cinematic frames using the Nano Banana 2 model, which costs zero credits. To turn those static frames into motion, the workflow applies the Omni Flash model set to a duration of 6 seconds.

For final assembly, Ai Lockup imports all rendered clips into the editing application [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/). That editing stage takes about 2 minutes to drop atmospheric horror music beneath the timeline, apply scene transitions, and export the finished project at 1080p resolution.
