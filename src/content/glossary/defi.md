DeFi represents a collection of financial services operating on public blockchains, primarily Ethereum, enabling users to lend, borrow, trade, and earn interest on cryptocurrencies directly, peer-to-peer. These applications utilize smart contracts, self-executing agreements whose terms are directly written into code, to automate transactions and enforce rules without the need for traditional financial institutions like banks or brokers. This architecture fosters an open financial system where anyone with an internet connection can access services, regardless of their geographical location or credit history.

The importance of DeFi stems from its potential to democratize finance and enhance transparency. By removing intermediaries, it can reduce operational costs and transaction fees, offering more efficient and accessible financial products. All transactions on a public blockchain are auditable by anyone, providing a high degree of transparency that is often absent in conventional finance. It also promotes censorship resistance, as no single entity can halt or reverse transactions once they are recorded on the blockchain. For example, a person can use a DeFi lending platform like Aave to deposit cryptocurrency and earn interest, or to borrow funds against their crypto collateral, all without a bank performing credit checks or holding their assets. This direct interaction with financial protocols exemplifies the core promise of decentralized finance.
