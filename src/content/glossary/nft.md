---
term: "NFT"
slug: "nft"
category: "Crypto"
definition: "An NFT, or Non-Fungible Token, is a unique digital identifier recorded on a blockchain that verifies ownership of a specific item or piece of content, which can be digital or physical."
---

An NFT represents a unique digital certificate of ownership for a particular asset, which could be an image, video, audio file, or even a physical object. Unlike cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, which are fungible and interchangeable, each NFT possesses distinct properties and cannot be directly swapped for another. Blockchain technology underpins NFTs, providing a public and immutable ledger that records transaction history and verifies the rightful owner of the token. This technology makes it possible to prove the scarcity and authenticity of digital items, a concept that was challenging to achieve before.

NFTs hold significance because they introduce verifiable digital ownership and scarcity to a world of easily replicated digital content. They empower creators by offering new methods to monetize their digital art, music, and other works directly with collectors, bypassing traditional intermediaries. This mechanism fosters new creator economies and facilitates new models for digital collectibles, gaming, and virtual identities. The transparency of blockchain records supports clear provenance and transfer of ownership. For example, artist Beeple sold his digital collage "Everydays: The First 5000 Days" as an NFT for $69 million in 2021. This event showcased the potential for digital art to attain substantial market value and for ownership to be verifiably tracked and transferred through a non-fungible token.
