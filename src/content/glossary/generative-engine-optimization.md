When someone asks an AI assistant a question, the answer is assembled from sources and often shown without a click. Traditional SEO competes for position in a list of links. GEO competes to be one of the sources the answer is built from.

The shift matters because the click may never happen. A page can influence thousands of answers and see almost none of that traffic, which breaks the measurement every SEO team is built around.

## What appears to help

The honest framing first: this is a young field, the systems are opaque, and much of what is sold as GEO expertise is guesswork with a price tag. The following are reasonable inferences, not established rules.

**Answer the question early and plainly.** Systems extracting a claim tend to take a clear sentence over a paragraph that circles the point.

**Make claims attributable.** Specific, checkable statements with a source are easier to quote than general assertions, and quoting is the mechanism.

**Structure the page.** Clear headings, question-shaped subheads, and a definition near the top give a retrieval system obvious units to pull.

**Be current.** Systems favour recent sources for anything time-sensitive, and there is more of that than people assume.

## What has not changed

The underlying requirement is the same as it has been: say something that is true, specific and not available in the same form on a hundred other pages. A model summarising ten interchangeable pages will cite whichever it happened to retrieve. A page with a number, a method or a first-hand observation gives it a reason.

Treat GEO as an extension of doing the work properly, not a separate discipline — and be sceptical of anyone selling a technique for it, since nobody outside the model providers can currently verify one.
