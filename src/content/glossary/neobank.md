Neobanks are financial institutions operating entirely online, providing banking services exclusively through digital platforms like mobile apps and websites, without physical branches. They leverage technology to offer a streamlined user experience, emphasizing efficiency and convenience. Their lack of physical infrastructure often means lower operating costs than traditional banks, allowing them to develop innovative features and target specific customer segments.

They matter by introducing significant competition and innovation within the financial sector. Neobanks offer alternatives to conventional banking, typically providing lower fees, faster account opening, and real-time transaction notifications. Their mobile-first design and user experience appeal to individuals seeking greater financial control and modern digital interactions. By simplifying access to financial services, neobanks satisfy a demand for agile banking solutions, prompting established institutions to enhance their digital offerings.

A concrete example is Revolut, a prominent UK-based neobank. Revolut provides users with diverse financial services, including current accounts, debit cards, and international money transfers, all managed through its mobile application. It offers real-time spending insights, multi-currency accounts, and fee-free currency exchange up to specific limits. This makes it popular for international transactions and illustrates how a digital-first approach attracts customers seeking convenience and advanced features.
